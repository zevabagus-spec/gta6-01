Solmar Heat is an original open-world crime game set in Solmar Bay, a sun-soaked procedural grid city with a beachfront, a downtown tower, an old market, a harbor and a marina. It is built with a hand-written vanilla WebGL engine — zero vendored libraries, zero network requests, every line written for this entry. One shader program draws the whole city: per-vertex colors, a warm directional sun, distance fog, and glow for lit windows, lamps and the gold mission beacon. The static city uploads once; cars, people, particles and pickups are re-emitted every frame.

You play Mara Voss, a driver who owes fixer Ilsa Marek one debt. Six story missions pay it off: an errand, a car theft, a corner fight, a timed harbor run under police heat, a shootout at Tower Plaza, and a final payday at Bay Trust Bank — each with cash rewards, progression and autosave. Between jobs the city is free roam: steal and drive five invented car models, dodge traffic and pedestrians, hunt 20 gold sun coins, and manage a 0–5 star wanted level against pursuit cars and foot officers who bust you if they corner you. Combat covers fists and a pistol with ammo, reloads and enemy drops; the HUD carries cash, health, a canvas minimap with a mission marker, wanted stars, speed and objectives; title, pause, busted, wasted, mission-complete and story-complete screens round it out, with procedural WebAudio sound effects and a synth music loop. Progress saves to localStorage with a validated, corruption-proof fallback.

## Run it

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open http://localhost:8080 — WASD to move and drive, E to steal or exit a car, F to punch, click to shoot, Q to swing the camera, P to pause.
